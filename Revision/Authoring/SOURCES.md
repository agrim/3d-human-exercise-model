# Choosing and fitting the body

The selection was driven by three things visible during a repetition. The viewer needs to recognize the posture, see a specific muscle change color without coloring its neighbors, and see a hand or a foot bear against the correct piece of equipment. No single source supplied those three, the app's exercise library, and an efficient native player.

| Source | What it contributed to the decision |
| --- | --- |
| BodyParts3D 4.0 | The IS-A archive supplies separate bilateral structures, including heads of the deltoids, long bones, phalanges and a corresponding outer skin. Their shared coordinates make them a good basis for joint pivots and floor and handle landmarks. This is the principal anatomy source. [Archive](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/download.html) · [License](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/lic.html) |
| Z-Anatomy | The first archive lacks the lats and rectus abdominis. We extracted those and three other paired trunk muscles from this atlas, then registered them against eight corresponding bones. The per-part IDs and adaptation license ship with the model. [Pinned original](https://github.com/Z-Anatomy/Models-of-human-anatomy/tree/7b9197672eea2ed44ae238d3cfbf38e39b7586a9) |
| MakeHuman / MPFB | The base body and game-skeleton weights can supply a continuous exterior, but the base mesh lacks identified muscle structures. [Upstream license and asset scope](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/blob/437dd513888a92399d1d3200d2e80859fae55abc/LICENSE.md) |
| Quaternius Universal Base Characters | The free pack offers rigged regular and superhero body types with convenient engine retargeting. Its character surfaces would need a muscle classification layer. [Publisher](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalbasecharacters.html) |
| Blender Studio realistic male and Snow | The realistic male is an editable sculpting base. Snow supplies a production character rig with a distinct visual style. Both are credible sources for exterior detail. Neither supplies the individually identified training muscles needed for the anatomy view. [Realistic base](https://studio.blender.org/training/realistic-human-research/use-of-base-meshes/) · [Snow rig](https://studio.blender.org/characters/snow/v4/) |
| Meta MHR and NVIDIA SOMA | Both are candidates for parametric body and skeleton fitting. Their strengths matter for fitting arbitrary people. The mobile task here is to replay named exercise demonstrations on a known skeleton with identifiable tissue. Baking those motions avoids shipping a solver or model weights. [MHR](https://github.com/facebookresearch/MHR) · [SOMA model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/SOMA-X/blob/main/README.md) |
| OpenSim model library | The arm and gait models provide useful joint and muscle-path references, including geometry for individual bones. They serve a biomechanics workflow. The visible muscle belly silhouettes needed for this product came from the two anatomical atlases. [OpenSim models and geometry](https://github.com/opensim-org/opensim-models) |

The dummy uses separate muscle and bone geometry from BodyParts3D and Z-Anatomy. The head, palms and feet retain matching exterior surfaces. Selected superficial fascia is recessed over the abdominal wall so that the real rectus can be seen in the crunch. A few named training regions project a deep or partly covered structure onto the nearby visible muscle surface. The [anatomy authoring notes](anatomy/README.md) document these choices.

The model credits and adaptation terms travel inside the Swift package. [Bundled notices](../Sources/ExerciseDummy/Resources/ATTRIBUTION.md)
